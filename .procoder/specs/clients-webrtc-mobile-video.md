# clients-webrtc-mobile-video

Status: approved (2026-10-25; the user's answers to the open questions are folded in, see "Decisions" at the end)

Source: the user's instruction of 2026-10-25 to add four things to Hello: (1) a WebRTC softphone in the web UI, (2) iOS and Android apps with push for incoming calls, (3) video calls, (4) Microsoft Teams integration. This reverses the WebRTC non-goal in `hello-pbx-spec.md` §3 ("Non-Goals": "... WebRTC, large-scale conferencing ...") and the "WSS/WebRTC later" note in §5 (item 1). Verification convention used throughout: **[V]** means the fact was read from the cited source on 2026-10-25; **[U]** means it is from general knowledge or a source that could not be fetched, and must be confirmed before the phase that relies on it; **[L]** means it needs a lab, a device or an external account. ## Problem

Hello serves SIP desk phones on a LAN. A user cannot take a call in a browser tab, on a phone away from the desk, or with video, and a company that lives in Microsoft Teams cannot reach Hello extensions or be reached from them. Four obstacles are specific to Hello's design and shape everything below:

1. **Hello anchors every call** (`incall-ha.md` S-7, `internal/sip/anchor.go` `AnchorPolicy`). That makes a WebRTC gateway cheap in architecture (the relay already sits between every pair of endpoints) and makes video a relay problem, not a "pass the SDP through" one.
2. **The relay is audio-only and plain RTP.** `internal/media/sdp.go` `ParseAudioSDP` keeps the first `m=audio` section and requires `RTP/AVP`; `BuildAudioSDP` emits one audio stream. A browser offer (`UDP/TLS/RTP/SAVPF`, ICE, DTLS fingerprint, BUNDLE, `rtcp-mux`) and a video offer both fall outside it. No SRTP, DTLS or ICE code exists yet (nothing under `internal/` mentions them).
3. **A phone that is not running cannot be rung.** Desk phones hold a registration. An iOS or Android app that is suspended has no socket, so Hello must wake it and only then fork the INVITE to it.
4. **Microsoft Teams Direct Routing is supported only through certified SBCs** [V], and Hello is not one.

## Users

- **Office users** take and place calls in the browser while at a computer, with the desk phone ringing at the same time (ring-all).
- **Mobile users** (not now, user decision 2026-10-25; the design is kept so the apps can be added later) receive calls on their phone like any other call (native incoming-call screen, lock screen, Bluetooth), on Wi-Fi or cellular, and place calls from their extension.
- **Video users** upgrade a call to video between browsers (apps later), and later with video-capable desk phones and door stations, and find the video in the call's recording.
- **Administrators** enrol and revoke clients, see which clients are registered, and set video and ring policy.
- **The operator** keeps one more service (TURN, LAN/VPN-facing for now) healthy, watches the storage that video recordings use, and sees the metrics for them. APNs/FCM credentials come only with the mobile apps (not now).
- **A Teams-using company** gets a documented, supportable way to connect Hello to Teams Phone.

## Defaults chosen for open points

The user named the goals and left design open. These defaults settle each design question; changing one is a spec edit. The five that needed the user's confirmation were answered on 2026-10-25 and are recorded in "Decisions" at the end; the defaults below already reflect them.

1. **Media gateway: Go-native, in the existing anchor.** The anchor terminates ICE-lite, DTLS-SRTP and SRTP using the pure-Go Pion modules (`pion/ice`, `pion/dtls`, `pion/srtp`, `pion/rtp`, `pion/rtcp`, `pion/sdp`, all MIT [V]) and relays plain RTP to G.711/G.722/Opus phones. rtpengine and an SFU were considered and rejected for the 1:1 case (see S-3). Approved 2026-10-25 (Decision 5). This amends the `phone-feature-parity.md` constraint "at most one new Go module for DTLS": the DTLS/ICE piece of S-16 and this spec are the same code, delivered once, with the module set named in Phase 1 and reviewed there.
2. **Signalling: SIP over WSS (RFC 7118), terminated by Kamailio.** Hello's SIP stack keeps speaking UDP (later TLS, S-14) to Kamailio's `hello` socket. Browsers and apps are therefore "phones that register through the edge" and need no WebSocket code in `hello-sip`.
3. **A client is a device.** Each browser profile and each app install is a row in `devices` with a `kind` (`web`, `mobile`) and an owning `user`. Several devices register one extension (the AOR), and the existing ring-all forks to all of them.
4. **Client SIP credentials are derived, not stored.** A time-limited HMAC-derived digest secret (the TURN REST pattern) means no per-client secret in PostgreSQL and no snapshot propagation delay on first REGISTER.
5. **Mobile stack (not now, user decision 2026-10-25): React Native with Expo development builds**, one TypeScript SIP/WebRTC core shared with the web softphone (JsSIP + `react-native-webrtc`), plus a small amount of native Swift/Kotlin for PushKit/CallKit and FCM/Telecom. Native-only is the fallback if the Phase 8 spike fails. Kept as the design for when the user asks for the apps; nothing in Phases 1 to 6b depends on it.
6. **Push (not now, user decision 2026-10-25) is sent directly by `hello-sip`** to APNs and FCM with the owner's own credentials. A call to a mobile device is "push, wait for the app to register, then fork", with the wait state in Valkey so any node can finish it.
7. **Mobile login (not now, with the apps) is OAuth 2.1 authorization code with PKCE** against the authorization server Hello already runs (`internal/oauth`), with QR pairing as a convenience for an already-logged-in web user.
8. **Distribution (not now, with the apps) is TestFlight internal testing and Google Play internal testing first** [V: 100 internal testers on each]. No public store listing in this spec.
9. **Video is relayed, never transcoded.** H.264 Constrained Baseline is the common denominator, VP8 is used between WebRTC endpoints, and a call with no common video codec continues as audio. Video v1 is web endpoints only. **Video is recorded** (user decision 2026-10-25): the relayed H.264/VP8 is depacketized without decoding into segmented MP4/WebM in MinIO next to the existing audio WAV (S-42, Phase 6b). Desk-phone and door video wait for hardware; conference-room video comes after conference rooms.
10. **TURN is coturn on kw**, one Deployment per public address, short-lived REST credentials (1 h), deployed by the same Kuvryn Sync pattern as everything else in `deploy/kuvryn-sync/kw`.
11. **Teams: no Teams signalling in Hello.** Direct Routing through a certified SBC (or a Direct-Routing-as-a-Service provider), with Hello seeing the SBC as an `ip`-mode trunk. Operator Connect, Teams Phone Mobile and SIP Gateway do not fit (S-47). Hello delivers only `docs/teams.md` and the SBC-peer trunk profile; nothing is built or bought until a tenant and a real need exist, and no certification is attempted (user decision 2026-10-25).
12. **Order of work:** S-13 to S-16 of `phone-feature-parity.md` (items 22 to 25) land before any WebRTC phase. `kamailio-ha.md` does not block this spec, but the WebSocket flow rule in S-10 is written so the two compose.
13. **Reach:** LAN and VPN first (user decision 2026-10-25). Internet exposure (public DNS name, public CA certificate, router port forwards, TURN reachable from cellular) is Phase 12, an optional later step the user decides once the apps work.
14. **Browsers:** current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. Per-browser video codec behaviour is a lab item **[L]**; the spec does not assume it.

## What exists today (read from the repo, `main` 8ac01c2)

- **Edge.** One Kamailio 6.0 (Debian trixie build, `docker/kamailio/Dockerfile`), `hostNetwork` on master-11, `192.168.10.101:30508` udp and tcp for phones and `5070` udp for Hello (`deploy/kamailio/kamailio.cfg`, `deploy/kuvryn-sync/kw/resources.yaml`). Modules loaded today include `tm`, `rr`, `dispatcher`, `path`, `usrloc`, `registrar`, `nathelper`, `pike`, `xhttp`, `xhttp_prom`; no `tls` or `websocket`. It adds Path with the phone's `received` address on REGISTER and uses `set_contact_alias()` / `handle_ruri_alias()` for NAT. `xhttp` serves metrics on a separate TCP socket and refuses HTTP on the phones socket.
- **Hello SIP.** UDP only (`internal/sip/server.go` `ServeUDP`; `b2bua.go` refuses `sips:`). Bindings live in Valkey (`livestate.Binding`, `Transport: "udp"`), at most 10 per AOR (`registrar.go` `maxContacts`). Digest auth checks stored `ha1_md5`/`ha1_sha256` from the snapshot (`digest.go`).
- **Media.** `internal/media/relay.go` is a two-leg RTP relay with sequence/timestamp/SSRC rewriting, symmetric RTP latching, DTMF detection and ports from `HELLO_RTP_PORT_MIN/MAX` (20000 to 21000). On kw hello-sip runs `hostNetwork`, so RTP lands on the node IP in `HELLO_MEDIA_ANCHOR_HOST`. Every call anchors (`AnchorPolicy`). Takeover re-INVITEs both endpoints to the surviving node's new ports (`incall-ha.md` S-4).
- **Codecs.** PR #144 (open): the callee is offered the caller's list filtered to G.722, Opus, PCMU, PCMA and telephone-event; the relay passes any negotiated payload type; no transcoding. Recording decodes G.711, G.722 and Opus.
- **Identity.** `users`, `sessions`, `api_tokens`, `extensions`, `devices` (one SIP username and digest HA1 per device, many devices per extension) in migration 00002. The `user-accounts` branch (migration 00026, unmerged) adds the `user` role, `user_extensions`, `/api/v1/me/*` and `must_change_password`. `internal/oauth` already provides an authorization server (PKCE, dynamic client registration or client-ID metadata documents, grants, revocation) for MCP clients.
- **Web.** React/Vite app in `web/` (pnpm), served by `hello-ui` behind ingress-nginx on `hello.kw.watteel.lab` with a cert-manager `cluster-ca` certificate (`resources.yaml` Ingress `hello`).
- **Trunks.** `trunks.mode` is `registration` or `ip` (migration 00003); `ip` trunks authenticate by `source_cidrs`.
- **Deployment.** Kuvryn Sync applies `deploy/kuvryn-sync/kw/`: images pinned `sha-<full sha>`, Kamailio pinned `kc-<hash>` of `docker/kamailio`, credentials in `secret-*.sops.yaml` with skeletons, `LoadBalancer` addresses from Cilium LB-IPAM (`192.168.10.12x` to `15x`). Arc runners are Linux; nothing runs on the Mac.

## In scope

### A. Shared media layer (extends S-15 and S-16 of phone-feature-parity)

- [S-1] **Prerequisite gate.** No phase below starts before these have merged, in this order: `phone-feature-parity.md` items 22 (S-13 TCP), 23 (S-14 TLS and `sips`, with the cert-manager certificate), 24 (S-15 SRTP SDES), 25 (S-16 DTLS-SRTP and ICE-lite); PR #144 (wideband, Opus passthrough); `user-accounts` (the `user` role and `/api/v1/me/*`). The WebRTC leg of S-3 is the S-16 DTLS/ICE machinery with a browser-shaped SDP in front of it; if S-16 is implemented first as specified there, Phase 1 here adds only the SDP profile and the browser interop tests, and if not, Phase 1 delivers S-16 and phone-feature-parity item 25 is closed by it.
- [S-2] **SDP model.** A new multi-section SDP model in `internal/media` (built on `pion/sdp/v3`) parses and builds sessions with audio and video sections, `a=group:BUNDLE`, `a=mid`, `a=rtcp-mux`, `a=rtcp-fb`, `a=extmap`, `a=fmtp`, ICE (`ufrag`, `pwd`, `candidate`, `ice-lite`, `end-of-candidates`), DTLS (`fingerprint`, `setup`) and SDES (`crypto`). `ParseAudioSDP` and `BuildAudioSDP` stay for the Hello-terminated media (voicemail, announcements, recording notice) and are re-implemented on the model where trivial. Property: parse then build of every vendor SDP in `internal/media/sdp_test.go` is a no-op on the audio section.
- [S-3] **Leg profiles and the gateway decision.** Every relay leg has a profile: `plain` (`RTP/AVP`), `sdes` (`RTP/SAVP`, S-15) or `webrtc` (`UDP/TLS/RTP/SAVPF`). The profile is chosen from the `m=` transport of the endpoint's SDP; the anchor builds the other leg's SDP in that leg's profile. Plain RTP between the relay's two legs stays as today (sequence/timestamp/SSRC rewriting, latching, DTMF); the `webrtc` and `sdes` legs differ only in a transport that decrypts inbound and encrypts outbound packets before and after the unchanged rewrite.
  Why this and not the alternatives:
  - **rtpengine** (the usual Kamailio companion) would bridge profiles but is a second RTP engine beside `internal/media`; `media-anchoring.md` keeps `internal/media` behind a Hello-owned interface, `incall-ha.md` replicates relay state and re-homes media on takeover, and recording, announcements, voicemail and DTMF detection all read relay packets. Two engines would split that. It would also need `hostNetwork` UDP ranges, a control channel and its own HA.
  - **An SFU (LiveKit, Janus, mediasoup)** solves multi-party, not bridging to plain-RTP phones; it is the later answer for conference video (S-45), not for 1:1 calls.
  - **Go-native in the anchor** keeps one media path, one metrics set, one HA model and `CGO_ENABLED=0`, at the price of owning ICE-lite, DTLS and SRTP code paths. The price is bounded: Pion implements the protocols and S-16 needs them anyway.
- [S-4] **ICE-lite and DTLS-SRTP termination.**
  - The anchor answers as ICE-lite (RFC 8445 §3.3 [U: section number]) with host candidates only: the node address from `HELLO_MEDIA_ANCHOR_HOST` and, for remote clients, `HELLO_MEDIA_WEBRTC_PUBLIC_IP`. WebRTC legs share one UDP port per node (`HELLO_MEDIA_WEBRTC_PORT`, default 21100, outside the 20000 to 21000 relay range) through a Pion UDP mux that demultiplexes by ICE ufrag; this keeps firewall rules and the TURN `allowed-peer-ip` list to one port per node **[L: confirm the mux works with a lite agent]**.
  - `rtcp-mux` and BUNDLE are required of WebRTC legs (RFC 8835 [U: section]); the plain-RTP leg uses the phone's own RTCP port as today.
  - DTLS role follows RFC 5763 negotiation. Each hello-sip process creates a fresh ECDSA P-256 certificate at start; the SHA-256 fingerprint goes in `a=fingerprint`. The certificate is not persisted and not shared between nodes: after a takeover the new owner has a new fingerprint, which forces the browser through a fresh DTLS handshake on the ICE restart (S-7) **[L: confirm Chrome, Firefox and Safari re-handshake on a changed fingerprint]**.
  - SRTP profiles offered: `SRTP_AEAD_AES_128_GCM` and `SRTP_AES128_CM_HMAC_SHA1_80` (what browsers support [U]); keys exist only in memory and are never logged.
  - The signalling channel is the trust anchor for the fingerprint (RFC 8827 [U]). WSS to Kamailio is TLS; Kamailio to hello-sip must therefore be TLS (S-12) before a WebRTC call is accepted in production.
- [S-5] **Codec policy: passthrough, no transcoding.** A browser or app must support Opus and PCMU/PCMA [V RFC 7874], and desk phones support G.711, so a common audio codec always exists between a WebRTC endpoint and any Hello phone and no transcoder is needed. Negotiation reuses the #144 rule (the callee is offered the caller's list filtered to G.722, Opus, PCMU, PCMA, telephone-event; the caller is answered with the callee's choice). Chrome and Firefox also offer G.722 **[L]**, which gives wideband to WP phones without Opus. RFC 4733 telephone-event passes through; browsers send DTMF with it.
- [S-6] **No Hello-terminated media on browser legs without S-4.** Voicemail, announcements, the recording notice and paging play to a WebRTC endpoint through the same leg transport (they are relay-side senders); `PlayTo` writes plaintext RTP into the leg's transport, which encrypts it.
- [S-7] **Takeover of WebRTC legs.** `incall-ha.md` S-4 re-INVITEs both endpoints to the new owner. For a `webrtc` leg the re-INVITE carries new ICE credentials and the new fingerprint (an ICE restart). The browser or app must accept a re-INVITE with a new offer; the shared client core (S-19) implements the `reinvite` handler. Target: the same 3 s claim-to-rehome as phones. If a client does not recover within 6 s of the node death, the call is ended with a BYE (CDR `ha: taken-over-failed`), not left as a zombie. This is **[L]** and is the least certain item in the HA story; the statement in `docs/ha.md` for WebRTC calls stays "best effort" until the lab passes.

### B. Edge: SIP over WSS in Kamailio

- [S-8] **Kamailio changes.** The Kamailio image adds `kamailio-websocket-modules` and `kamailio-tls-modules` [V: packages exist in trixie] and the build-time config check loads `websocket.so` and `tls.so`. The config adds a TLS listener for WSS, loads `websocket` with a ping keepalive (module parameters `keepalive_mechanism`, `keepalive_timeout`, `keepalive_interval` [V: listed]), accepts the `sip` sub-protocol (RFC 7118), and handles the HTTP upgrade in `event_route[xhttp:request]` for the WSS socket only (the phones and metrics sockets keep refusing HTTP). Requests are accepted only if `Origin` equals the console origin (`HELLO_PUBLIC_URL`) or an allow-list; a WebSocket from any other origin is closed (cross-site WebSocket hijacking). **[U: the exact script lines; the lab config proves them.]**
- [S-9] **Exposure.** Browsers and apps connect to `wss://hello.kw.watteel.lab/sip`, a new Ingress path on the existing host, so they reuse the console's certificate, need no second DNS name and no CORS. The nginx Ingress sets `nginx.ingress.kubernetes.io/backend-protocol: "HTTPS"` and proxies to a ClusterIP Service `kamailio-ws` whose endpoint is Kamailio's WSS listener (`hostNetwork`, so the endpoint is the node address), with long read/send timeouts. A Cilium NetworkPolicy admits only the ingress controller to that port. Kamailio sees the ingress pod as the TCP peer and takes the client address for logging and rate limits from `X-Forwarded-For` of that trusted peer only. If the Ingress path proves unreliable for WebSockets **[L]**, the fallback is a `LoadBalancer` Service on a LB-IPAM address with the same certificate mounted in Kamailio.
- [S-10] **Contact, Path and Record-Route for WebSocket clients.**
  - A browser's Contact host is a random `.invalid` name (RFC 7118 [U]). On REGISTER and on dialog-forming requests Kamailio rewrites it with `set_contact_alias()` (the existing mechanism; alias protocol id for ws/wss) so Hello and later requests reach the live connection.
  - Path and the phone-facing Record-Route for a `ws`/`wss` contact name **this Kamailio instance's own address**, not the HA virtual address of `kamailio-ha.md`. A WebSocket is a TCP connection pinned to one instance; a request for it sent to the other instance has no connection to use. When that instance dies the client sees the socket close, reconnects through the Ingress to the survivor and re-REGISTERs (RFC 7118 behaviour; JsSIP reconnects automatically **[L]**); until then the binding is stale and Hello forks to its other devices.
  - `nathelper` keepalive OPTIONS are skipped for ws/wss contacts (the WebSocket ping replaces them).
- [S-11] **Trust is unchanged.** `HELLO_SIP_TRUSTED_PROXIES` already covers the Kamailio addresses; hello-sip sees UDP from Kamailio's `hello` socket and needs no WebSocket code.
- [S-12] **Kamailio to hello-sip over TLS.** The `hello` socket gains the TLS transport of S-14 (the cert-manager CA signs both ends) and the dispatcher uses `tls:` destinations for WebRTC-originated traffic. Until that is on, WebRTC calls are accepted only with `HELLO_MEDIA_WEBRTC_ALLOW_CLEARTEXT_HOP=true` (default false), because the DTLS fingerprint in the SDP is only as trustworthy as the path that carries it. Cilium transparent encryption on kw is **[U]** and, if present, is documented as an alternative.

### C. Devices, identity and registration

- [S-13] **Device kinds.** `devices` gains `kind` (`desk`, `web`, `mobile`; existing rows are `desk`), `user_id` (owner, required for `web` and `mobile`), `label`, `platform`, `app_version`, `last_seen_at` and `auth_mode` (`digest`, `derived`). Many devices per extension is already supported; this adds ownership and a way to list and revoke them. An extension can have desk phones and clients at once, and a user can have several clients (default cap 5, `HELLO_CLIENT_MAX_DEVICES`).
- [S-14] **Derived credentials.** For `derived` devices the SIP username is `<w|m><deviceId>.<expiryUnix>` (matches the existing `^[A-Za-z0-9._-]{1,64}$` rule). The digest secret is `base64url(HMAC-SHA256(HELLO_CLIENT_AUTH_KEY, "<username>:<realm>"))`; hello-sip computes the HA1 values on the fly (`digest.go` gains a second HA1 source next to the snapshot) and rejects an expired `expiryUnix` as a bad credential (the client then refreshes through the API). No client secret is stored, so a new web session can REGISTER the moment the API returns, with no snapshot delay. Lifetimes: web 12 h, mobile 30 d; the client refreshes through the authenticated API (S-27), including in the push-wake path with the stored refresh token. Revocation sets `devices.enabled=false` (it reaches hello-sip in the snapshot within its refresh interval) and drops the bindings. `HELLO_CLIENT_AUTH_KEY` is a sops secret with a current and a previous value so a key change does not drop registered clients. Digest algorithm stays SHA-256 first, MD5 second, as today.
- [S-15] **Registration and forking.**
  - A client REGISTERs its device (username above) for the extension's AOR with a normal digest REGISTER over WSS. The Contact carries `+sip.instance` and `reg-id` (RFC 5626) so a reconnect replaces the same binding instead of adding one (JsSIP can send them [U]). Expiry is 120 s for foreground web clients and re-sent at half-life; WebSocket ping failures remove the binding sooner.
  - The call flow forks to every live binding of the extension (ring-all, as `callflow.go` does), whatever the kind. The cap `maxContacts` stays 10 per AOR and becomes configurable (`HELLO_SIP_MAX_CONTACTS`); a client binding replaces its own previous binding (same `+sip.instance`) rather than counting twice.
  - User preference (`users.ring_web`, `users.ring_mobile`, both default true, `/api/v1/me/profile`): a kind set to false is skipped when forking. Do-not-disturb, forwarding and schedules apply before any fork, as today.
  - A mobile device with a push binding but no live foreground binding is handled by the push flow (S-31), not by this fork.
- [S-16] **Answered or declined elsewhere.** When one branch answers, every other branch gets CANCEL with `Reason: SIP;cause=200;text="Call completed elsewhere"` (RFC 3326 [U]) and clients show the call as answered elsewhere (CallKit `answeredElsewhere` [V]). A branch that declines (486 or 603 from a client "decline") ends only that branch; the call fails to the next stage only when no branch is left.
- [S-17] **CDR and trace.** The CDR leg records device kind and id, codec, whether video was negotiated (`cdrs.video`), the ICE candidate type of a WebRTC leg (host, relay) and, for a push-woken branch, the wake time. The routing trace gains steps: "push sent (apns)", "device registered after push (1.8 s)", "branch timed out waiting for registration".

### D. Web softphone

- [S-18] **UX.** A persistent softphone dock in the console shell (`web/src/App.tsx` layout), collapsed to a status chip (registered, unregistered, in call). States and behaviour:
  - **Enable**: a switch "Use this browser as a phone" per browser profile (stored in `localStorage` with a generated device id). First enable asks the browser for microphone permission with an explanatory screen, creates the device (S-24), fetches credentials and ICE servers, opens WSS and registers. Camera permission is asked on the first video action, not at enable.
  - **One active tab**: the tab that holds a Web Lock (`navigator.locks`, MDN Web Locks API [U]) is the phone; other tabs of the console show a read-only chip "Phone active in another tab" and can request the lock. This prevents two registrations and double ringing from one browser.
  - **Incoming call**: a toast with caller number, name (extension directory and contacts), the called extension, ring tag and a video badge; Answer, Answer with video, Decline. A ringtone plays only after the user has interacted with the page once (browser autoplay policy [U]); if the tab is hidden, a `Notification` (permission requested at enable) with Answer/Decline actions brings it forward. Ringing stops on CANCEL with the reason shown ("answered on Desk phone").
  - **In call**: mute, hold/resume (re-INVITE `sendonly`/`sendrecv`, as hello's hold handling expects), DTMF pad (RFC 4733), blind and attended transfer (REFER, handled by `internal/sip/transfer.go`), add call (second line, call waiting), video on/off (S-43), audio output and input device pickers (`enumerateDevices`, `setSinkId` where supported), a call-quality indicator from `getStats()` (loss, jitter, RTT, candidate type), keyboard shortcuts (answer, hang up, mute), full keyboard and screen-reader operability.

  - **Failure states** are visible: no microphone, permission denied, WSS closed (reconnecting with backoff), registration refused (credentials refreshed once, then an error), ICE failed (offers "retry call" and shows whether TURN was tried).
- [S-19] **Shared client core.** The SIP/WebRTC logic lives in a TypeScript package `clients/softphone-core` (pnpm workspace root `pnpm-workspace.yaml` listing `web`, `clients/*`), consumed by the web app and the mobile app. It defines `PhoneEngine` (register, unregister, call, answer, hold, transfer, dtmf, video, re-INVITE handling, events) over a `Platform` interface (WebSocket, RTCPeerConnection factory, media devices, storage). JsSIP is the SIP library: MIT, current release 3.13.9, pushed in October 2026 [V]; SIP.js is MIT but its last release is 2022 [V] and is not chosen. The core exposes no JsSIP types, so a library change is local. The `hello-ui` Docker build is updated to build the workspace (Phase 5).
- [S-20] **Call quality report.** At call end the client posts a summary to `POST /api/v1/me/calls/{callId}/quality` (codec, loss, jitter, RTT, candidate types, video resolution and frame rate). The server attaches it to the CDR; it never contains media.

### E. NAT traversal: STUN and TURN (coturn on kw)

- [S-21] **Deployment.** coturn runs from a new image `docker/coturn/Dockerfile` built from Debian trixie's `coturn` package [V: 4.6.1-2 exists], the same reason Kamailio is built from Debian (native arm64, kw nodes are arm64), tagged like Kamailio with a content tag (`ct-<hash of docker/coturn>`) so unrelated deploys do not restart it. - [S-23] **Credentials.** `GET /api/v1/me/ice-servers` (any authenticated user) returns STUN and TURN URIs for both servers, `username = "<expiryUnix>:<deviceId>"`, `credential = base64(HMAC-SHA1(HELLO_TURN_SECRET, username))`, TTL 3600 s (the TURN REST scheme [V draft]). Clients refetch before each call when the credential is older than half its life. LAN calls normally never allocate on TURN (host candidates connect). The endpoint is rate limited and audited by count, not per credential.

### G. Push gateway (wake the app, then fork) (not now, user decision 2026-10-25)

> **Not now (user decision 2026-10-25).** Push exists only to wake the mobile apps, so it waits for them. S-30 to S-35 stay as the design; the forking code of S-15 keeps the "mobile device with a push binding" branch point so the push flow can be added without reshaping it.

- [S-30] **Push bindings.** An app registers its push token with `PUT /api/v1/me/devices/{id}/push {provider: "apns"|"fcm", token, apnsEnv, appId}` at launch and on every token change. Tokens are stored sealed with `HELLO_SECRET_KEY` (`push_bindings`, Data) and carried in the configuration snapshot to hello-sip (no PostgreSQL call on the SIP path); they are never logged and never returned by the API. A provider answer that says the token is dead (APNs `410 Unregistered`/`BadDeviceToken`, FCM `UNREGISTERED`) deletes the binding and marks the device "push broken" in the UI.
  Hello-specific by design: Hello owns both ends, so a token update over HTTPS is simpler and safer than RFC 8599 `pn-*` Contact parameters [V: RFC 8599 defines them]. Support for third-party clients using RFC 8599 is a later, additive phase.
- [S-31] **Call flow for a mobile device.** The caller's INVITE reaches hello-sip node N through Kamailio.
  1. N builds the ring set. Live foreground bindings fork immediately as today. For each `mobile` device of the extension whose user has `ring_mobile` and that has a push binding but **no live foreground binding**, N creates a wait slot and continues.
  2. N sends `180 Ringing` to the caller as soon as at least one branch exists or a push was dispatched, so the caller hears ringback while the app wakes.
  3. N sends the push (S-33) with the Hello call id, caller number and name, video flag and a 30 s expiry, after a Valkey `SET hello:push:sent:{callId}:{deviceId} NX EX 60` that suppresses a duplicate push if a retry or a failover re-runs this INVITE.
  4. The app wakes, reports the call to CallKit/Telecom at once (it can be answered before the network is ready), opens WSS, REGISTERs with an `X-Hello-Wake: <callId>` header and its `fg` Contact parameter set.
  5. The REGISTER lands on any node M. M stores the binding, then looks up wait slots for that device (`hello:push:wait:{deviceId}`) and publishes the device id on the Valkey channel `hello:push:wake`. The node that owns the call (N) is subscribed, matches its waiting branch and forks a new INVITE to the fresh contact with `X-Hello-Call: <callId>` so the app can match it to the CallKit call. M's `200 OK` to the REGISTER carries `X-Hello-Wake-Result: ringing | ended | answered-elsewhere` so the app can drop a CallKit call that is already over.
  6. If the user answered in the native UI before the INVITE arrived, the app answers it as soon as it arrives (the app holds the intent). If the user declined, the app sends `POST /api/v1/me/calls/{callId}/decline` (or declines the INVITE when it arrives); only that branch ends.
  7. If no REGISTER arrives within the wait (default the ring timeout, `HELLO_PUSH_WAIT`), the branch ends as no answer, the trace says why, and the call proceeds to its next stage.
- [S-32] **HA.** The wait slot is `hello:push:wait:{deviceId}` (a hash of `callId -> owner node`, TTL ring timeout plus 5 s) in Valkey; the wake channel is Valkey pub/sub. Any node can accept the REGISTER and any node owning a waiting call forks. If the owner node dies during the wait, the INVITE at Kamailio fails over (the existing `HELLO_FAILOVER` route) to another node, which re-runs the call; the NX key stops a second push, the wait slot is re-created for the new owner, and the app, which uses a UUID derived from the original caller Call-ID, sees one call. If Valkey is down, the push still goes out (it needs no Valkey) but a failover cannot be deduplicated; the app dedupes by call id and reports a second push for a known call, then ends it immediately **[L: confirm CallKit accepts this without penalty]**. A restarted app whose push arrives after the call ended reports it and ends it with `remoteEnded` or `unanswered` [V reasons] on the REGISTER result.
- [S-33] **Providers.** A new `internal/push` package with a `Provider` interface (`Send(ctx, binding, payload) (Result, error)`) and two implementations:
  - **APNs** over HTTP/2 with token authentication (ES256 JWT from the owner's `.p8` key, key id and team id), `apns-push-type: voip`, `apns-topic: <bundle id>.voip`, `apns-priority: 10`, `apns-expiration` 0 or a few seconds so a stale call is never delivered later [V: header values; Apple's page states the expiration advice]. The VoIP payload limit is 5120 bytes [V]. Provider-token refresh cadence is **[U]** (Apple limits how often a token may be regenerated; the client caches the JWT and renews it on a timer well inside an hour). Environments: `sandbox` for development builds and TestFlight/production builds use their own environment value stored per binding.
  - **FCM** HTTP v1 (`messages:send`) with a service-account OAuth token, a **data-only** message with `android.priority: HIGH` and a short `ttl` **[U: v1 field names]**. Data-only so the app, not the system tray, controls the incoming-call UI.
  - Per-provider circuit breaker, bounded concurrency, timeouts of 2 s, one retry on connection errors only, and metrics (S-52). The sender runs in hello-sip; a push request never blocks call processing (it runs on its own goroutine, like the presence publisher).
  - Libraries: standard `net/http` with HTTP/2 and `golang.org/x/net`, plus `github.com/golang-jwt/jwt/v5` and `golang.org/x/oauth2/google` if not already present **[U]**; the project reviews them in Phase 7. `sideshow/apns2` (MIT, last push 2025-07-22 [V]) is an acceptable alternative.
  - Credentials: `HELLO_PUSH_APNS_*` and `HELLO_PUSH_FCM_*` from sops secrets; the `.p8` key and service-account JSON never reach the database, logs or the API.
- [S-34] **Payload and privacy.** The push carries `{v, callId, from, fromName, to, video, ts, exp}` and no tokens or credentials. Caller number and name pass through Apple or Google; `HELLO_PUSH_INCLUDE_CALLER` (default true, because CallKit needs something to show immediately) can be turned off, in which case the app shows "Incoming call" and updates the CallKit call from the REGISTER result.
- [S-35] **Missed calls.** When a woken app never registered (offline), a normal (non-VoIP) notification "Missed call from X" is sent after the ring timeout through APNs alert / FCM notification so the user knows, and the call appears in `/me/calls`. Optional in v1.1.

### H. Video

- [S-36] **Policy.** Hello relays video RTP in the anchor, never transcodes it, and never decodes it. A call starts with audio only unless the caller's offer has a video section. `extensions.video_allowed` (default true) and `HELLO_VIDEO_ENABLED` (default true once the phase ships) gate it. Trunk legs never get video: Hello answers a trunk's video section with `m=video 0` and strips any video section from a trunk-bound offer, so carriers see audio only.
- [S-37] **Codec negotiation.** Order of preference offered: H.264 Constrained Baseline (`profile-level-id=42e01f`, `packetization-mode=1`, `level-asymmetry-allowed=1`) then VP8. Browsers must implement both [V RFC 7742], which makes H.264 CB the common denominator with desk phones and doors, and VP8 a WebRTC-only option that never reaches a phone that lacks it. The video `fmtp` of the answerer is passed to the offerer as sent; the anchor does not rewrite codec parameters. If the two ends share no video codec or `profile-level-id` the video section is answered `m=video 0` and the call proceeds as audio, with a trace step and `hello_video_negotiation_failures_total`. H.265 and AV1 are not offered. Per-browser and per-device H.264 behaviour (Safari, Firefox, hardware encoders) is **[L]**.
- [S-38] **Relay mechanics.** For each video stream the anchor runs a second relay session per leg pair using the same leg transport as audio (S-3) and the same rewrite (constant sequence offset, timestamp offset, one SSRC per leg), so a retransmission request on one leg maps to a sequence number on the other with a constant shift. It is an RTP translator/mixer-style endpoint on each leg (RFC 7667 topologies [V exists]): RTCP is terminated and regenerated per leg (sender/receiver reports from the leg's own statistics), not passed raw. There is no jitter buffer and no repacketization.
- [S-39] **RTCP feedback.** The relay forwards picture loss indication (PLI), full intra request (FIR) and generic NACK from one leg to the other (NACK mapped by the sequence offset), and requests a keyframe from the sender when a leg is established, re-latched, re-homed by a takeover, or a stream starts after a hold. Bandwidth feedback: `goog-remb` is forwarded as the minimum of the receiver's estimate and the configured cap; transport-wide congestion control (`transport-cc`) and `abs-send-time` header extensions are not negotiated through the relay, because their sequence space is per hop, so the sender falls back to REMB and RTCP receiver reports **[L: quality of this fallback in each browser]**. Per-call ceiling `HELLO_VIDEO_MAX_KBPS` (default 1500) is written into `b=AS` and enforced as a rate cap on the relay (excess packets dropped, counted).
- [S-40] **Upgrade and downgrade.** A call may add or remove video mid-call by re-INVITE; the anchor adds or removes the video relay session live without touching audio. Hold stops video and audio together. The takeover of a call with video re-homes both streams and requests keyframes.
- [S-41] **Capacity.** A 720p call is about 1.5 Mbit/s each way, so about 3 Mbit/s through the node per call for the relay (one packet in, one out) **[U: assumption to measure in Phase 6]**. The per-node limit is the existing session limit plus `HELLO_VIDEO_MAX_SESSIONS` (default 20 per node); beyond it a video section is answered `m=video 0` and counted. A benchmark in Phase 6 sets the real number.
- [S-42] **Recording video (user decision 2026-10-25: record video).** When a call is recorded (`*1` or `record_default`) and has negotiated video, the video is recorded too, next to the existing audio recording. Nothing is decoded or transcoded.
  - **What is written.** The audio stays exactly as today (`internal/media/record.go`: decoded, mixed, one WAV per recording part in bucket `hello-recordings`). Each video stream (one per direction per leg pair, so normally two) is written as its own video-only file: H.264 into **fragmented MP4** (ISO BMFF, `avc1`, CMAF-style init segment plus `moof`/`mdat` fragments), VP8 into **WebM** (Matroska, `V_VP8`, one Cluster per fragment). The recorder depacketizes RTP without decoding (H.264 per RFC 6184: single NAL, STAP-A, FU-A; VP8 per RFC 7741 payload descriptor), reorders within a small window by sequence number, starts each file on a keyframe (it requests one with PLI/FIR when recording starts, which S-39 already does on join and re-home) and drops frames until the next keyframe after a loss it cannot repair. Timestamps come from the RTP timestamp (90 kHz); the wall-clock offset of the first frame relative to the audio recording start is taken from the anchor's receive time (and RTCP sender reports when present) and stored in the manifest. Audio is not muxed into the video files: G.711/G.722 would need encoding to fit MP4/WebM, which the no-transcoding rule forbids; the UI synchronises the two (below). A single-file export (audio and video muxed) is a later option and is not designed here.
  - **Segmented, streamed to MinIO.** Unlike audio (held in memory up to 10 MB and uploaded at the end), video is too large to hold: each stream is flushed as a numbered fragment object every 10 s or 4 MiB, whichever comes first (`HELLO_RECORDING_VIDEO_SEGMENT`), so memory per recorded video call stays under about 10 MiB. Keys: `rec/<unix>-<callid>/v<part>-<leg>-<dir>.<mp4|webm>/init` and `.../<seq>`, plus `rec/<unix>-<callid>/manifest.json` (codec, container, resolution from the SPS/VP8 header, per-stream start offset in ms against the audio, segment list with byte sizes, part number). When the call ends, a finalizer job in hello-control (claimed with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, any replica) concatenates init and fragments of each stream into one object `.../v<part>-<leg>-<dir>.<mp4|webm>` with a seek index (MP4 `sidx`, WebM `Cues`), verifies the size, and deletes the fragments. Until it runs (or if it fails), the API serves the fragments concatenated in order, which is a valid progressive file for both containers, only without fast seeking.
  - **Muxing libraries.** Pure Go, `CGO_ENABLED=0`: Pion's RTP depacketizers (`pion/rtp/codecs`, already in the Pion set), and an MP4 and a WebM writer. Candidates `github.com/Eyevinn/mp4ff` (MIT **[U]**) and `github.com/at-wat/ebml-go` (Apache-2.0 **[U]**), or small in-tree writers for the few boxes/elements needed; the choice is made and reviewed in Phase 6b.
  - **Policy and consent.** `HELLO_RECORDING_VIDEO` (default true once Phase 6b ships) and `extensions.record_video` (default true) gate it; both must allow it, and recording must already be on. A call that adds video mid-recording starts video files at that point (the manifest offset places them); removing video closes them. When video is recorded, the recording notice (`HELLO_MEDIA_RECORDING_NOTICE`) plays the "recorded including video" announcement variant, and the web softphone shows a recording badge with a camera mark on both sides that are Hello clients. Trunk legs never carry video (S-36), so nothing changes for external callers.
  - **Storage size.** Video dominates. At the default per-call ceiling `HELLO_VIDEO_MAX_KBPS=1500` per direction, one hour of a two-way video call is up to about 1.35 GB (2 x 1.5 Mbit/s x 3600 s / 8), against about 58 MB for an hour of 8 kHz 16-bit mono WAV or about 115 MB at 16 kHz; real calls usually sit below the ceiling **[U: measured in Phase 6b]**. So video has its own retention and quota: `recording_settings.video_retention_days` (default 30, 1 to 3650; audio retention is unchanged) purged by a hello-control job under `TryLock` (objects first, then rows, counts only in the audit), and `HELLO_RECORDING_VIDEO_QUOTA_GB` (default 200): hello-control keeps the running total in `recording_streams.bytes`; at 80 percent an alert fires, at 100 percent new video recordings do not start (audio recording continues, the trace says "video not recorded: quota") until the purge or the operator frees space. The docs give the sizing table above and recommend a MinIO capacity check before enabling it.
  - **Playback in the UI.** The call's recording view (Calls page, and `/me/calls` for the user's own calls under the existing recording access rules) shows a player: the remote party's video large, the other direction picture-in-picture, the existing WAV as the master clock. Each `<video>` element is muted and its `currentTime` is slaved to the audio element's time minus the manifest offset (resynced on seek, play and every 2 s if drift exceeds 100 ms). Parts from a takeover play one after another with a "recording gap" marker. `GET /api/v1/recordings/{id}/manifest` returns the manifest; `GET /api/v1/recordings/{id}/video/{stream}` streams the finalized object (or the concatenated fragments) with HTTP Range support, `Content-Type` `video/mp4` or `video/webm`, `Cache-Control: private, no-store`; the same role checks as audio recordings apply, and every video download is audited like audio. Browser support for H.264 in MP4 and VP8 in WebM across Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari is **[L]**; a stream the browser cannot play is offered as a download.
  - **HA during takeover.** The replicated dialog record (`incall-ha.md` S-1) gains the video recording state: stream ids, codec, part number and the last flushed fragment sequence. On takeover the taker starts a new part for audio (as today) and for each video stream (new init segment, first keyframe requested as part of the re-home), under the same correlation id, so the recording continues with a gap. What is lost: on a crash, the dead node's unflushed fragment (at most 10 s or 4 MiB per stream) plus the takeover gap; on a graceful handoff (drain) the owner flushes its open fragments before yielding, so only the re-home gap is lost. The finalizer treats each part separately; a part whose last fragment never arrived is finalized from what exists. If MinIO is unreachable, the recorder buffers at most 3 fragments per stream, then stops the video recording for that call (audio continues, trace "video recording stopped: object store"), counted in `hello_recording_video_failures_total{reason}`.
- [S-43] **Client video UX.** Web now (mobile later, with the apps): a video toggle in the call controls (adds or removes video by re-INVITE), self-view picture-in-picture, camera selection and front/back switch on mobile, a "bandwidth saving: audio only" choice, resolution and bitrate shown in the stats overlay, and video disabled for a call to a destination that cannot do it (the button says why).
- [S-44] **Desk phones and door stations.** This extends `phone-feature-parity.md` S-49 ("the anchor relays the video port pair unchanged"): with the relay of S-38 a plain-RTP video phone or door station interoperates with WebRTC endpoints and with other video phones. Hello cannot assume any lab phone does video (the WP822/WP836 in the lab are audio devices); every video-phone and door-station behaviour is **[L: hardware]** and no phase below depends on it.
- [S-45] **Conference-room video (later).** The `conference-rooms` branch mixes audio on the anchor. Video in rooms needs either last-active-speaker forwarding through the same relay (one video stream out, no mixing, no transcoding) or an SFU. This spec does not decide it; Phase 11 writes the amendment after `conference-rooms` has merged, with LiveKit (Apache-2.0 [V], Go, Pion-based) and a Pion-based in-process forwarder as the two candidates.
- [S-46] **Interop limits stated up front.** WebRTC-to-WebRTC video through Hello uses VP8 or H.264 end to end, whichever both support; a WebRTC endpoint calling a plain-RTP video phone needs a shared H.264 profile; a phone offering only H.263/H.265 gets audio. Nothing in Hello changes the picture.

## Out of scope

- Transcoding of audio or video; an SFU for 1:1 calls; rtpengine, Janus or FreeSWITCH.
- Muxing audio into the video recording files, and transcoding recordings to another format (S-42 keeps audio WAV and video MP4/WebM side by side); conference video before Phase 11; screen sharing (a second video stream; the relay could carry it, but UX and negotiation are not designed here).
- Chat and presence in the clients; SMS; Teams features beyond PSTN-style calls (S-50); implementing Direct Routing or Operator Connect.
- A public app-store listing, a hosted multi-tenant push relay, and Web Push (VAPID) for browsers. The interface in S-33 keeps a relay mode possible.
- Third-party SIP clients using RFC 8599 push parameters (additive later).
- **Not now (user decision 2026-10-25):** native iOS and Android apps, the push gateway and mobile video (sections F and G, old Phases 7, 8 and 9). The design stays in this spec so they can be added when the user asks.
- **Not now (user decision 2026-10-25):** Internet reach (Phase 12), until the user decides it once the clients work.
- Building or buying anything for Teams (an SBC, a DRaaS contract, a tenant) and any Microsoft certification attempt.
- Emergency calling from mobile apps and browsers: Hello does not route emergency calls from a client whose location it cannot know; the clients block emergency numbers with a message to use a phone. (`phone-feature-parity.md` S-28 covers desk phones.)
- Changes to other repositories (talking-agent and others). No deployment is part of this spec.

## Constraints

- No PostgreSQL call on the SIP path: device kinds, derived-credential state, ring preferences and sealed push tokens ride the configuration snapshot; wait slots and push dedupe live in Valkey.
- Every new runtime state works from any hello-sip replica and any Kamailio instance (S-10, S-32); losing one of either must not lose a registered client for longer than a reconnect.
- Security: SIP passwords, TURN secrets, pairing codes, push tokens, APNs keys and FCM credentials are sealed or sops-managed, never logged, never in metric labels, never in the snapshot in clear beyond process memory, never returned after creation. The authorization server grants `softphone` only to the clients that request it. Cross-origin WebSockets are refused (S-8).
- Performance: push dispatch begins within 100 ms of the INVITE reaching the node; wake to ringing target p95 under 5 s on iOS and Android with a warm network **[L]**; the SRTP/DTLS path adds under 50 µs per packet p99 in the benchmark of `phone-feature-parity.md`; video relay forwards a packet in under 1 ms p99 (benchmark in Phase 6).
- Dependencies: the Pion set named above (MIT, pure Go, `CGO_ENABLED=0` preserved), `pion/sdp`, an MP4 and a WebM writer for video recording (Phase 6b, or in-tree); JavaScript: JsSIP. Later, with the apps (not now): a JWT library and Google OAuth for FCM if absent, `react-native-webrtc`, `react-native-callkeep`, Expo. Each is named in the phase that adds it and reviewed. The phone-feature-parity limit of one new Go module for DTLS is amended by this spec as stated in Default 1.
- Deployment: kw via Kuvryn Sync as in `deploy/kuvryn-sync/README.md`; images content-tagged where inputs are narrow; no workloads on the user's Mac; iOS release builds on a macOS runner used only by the release workflow; CI on the Arc runners for everything else.
- Gate per PR: gofmt, `go vet`, golangci-lint (0), `go test -race ./...` green, `procoder check` 0 blocking, web lint and tests, mutation checks; REVIEW.md applies.

## Edge cases

- Two browser tabs and one profile: one registration (Web Lock); closing the lock holder hands the lock to another tab, which registers.
- The same user on web and mobile and a desk phone: all three ring; the first to answer wins and the rest get CANCEL with the reason; CallKit shows "answered elsewhere".
- A call arrives while the app is already in the foreground with a live binding: no push, direct fork; the app still reports it to CallKit.
- Push arrives after the call ended: the app reports and immediately ends it (`unanswered` or `remoteEnded`), as Apple requires a report for every VoIP push [V]; the REGISTER result tells it the state.
- The user declines in the native UI before the network is up: the decline is queued and sent over REST; the branch ends.
- Two pushes for one call (failover): the NX key prevents most; the UUID derived from the call id coalesces the rest.
- A device is revoked while ringing: its branch is cancelled by the snapshot update and its binding removed.
- Token expiry mid-call: the call continues (media is already up); the client refreshes before its next REGISTER.
- Reconnect storms (Kamailio restarts, all browsers reconnect): clients back off with jitter; Kamailio `pike` and the per-IP limits stay on; registrations re-use `reg-id` so bindings replace, not accumulate.
- A browser behind a corporate firewall that blocks UDP: ICE falls to TURN over TLS 5349; if that is blocked too the call fails with a visible "network blocks media" message (LAN-only deployments rarely hit this).
- Mixed-content or untrusted CA: the page cannot get microphone access over an untrusted HTTPS origin; the dock shows a specific message pointing to the CA install (S-51).
- Hold of a video call: both streams go inactive; resume requests a keyframe.
- A phone transfers a WebRTC caller (REFER): works through the existing transfer code because the browser leg is just another relay leg; the new target leg negotiates its own profile.
- Voicemail and announcements for a WebRTC caller: play through the leg transport (S-6).
- Codec mismatch: an Opus-only WebRTC endpoint calling a G.722-only phone has no common codec; Hello answers 488 with a clear trace (browsers always offer G.711, so this occurs only with a restricted client).
- DTMF `*1` recording works on WebRTC legs (RFC 4733 detection on the decrypted stream).
- IPv6-only clients: ICE host candidates of the anchor are IPv4; IPv6 is not offered in v1.

## Failure modes

- **Kamailio instance dies:** its WebSocket clients reconnect to a survivor and re-register; calls through it keep media and, per S-10, in-dialog signalling for that client resumes after the reconnect; a call in progress for a WebRTC client has a signalling gap until the reconnect (BYE is retried by the client).
- **hello-sip node dies:** phones behave as in `incall-ha.md`; WebRTC legs recover by ICE restart or the call ends cleanly (S-7).
- **Valkey down:** registrations fail as they do for phones (the existing `stateDown` 503); pushes still go out; wait slots cannot be matched, so a push-woken branch times out and the call falls to the next stage.
- **APNs or FCM down or slow:** the circuit breaker opens, calls ring the other devices, the trace says "push provider unavailable", and `hello_push_sent_total{result="error"}` alerts.
- **Dead push token:** the binding is removed on the provider's answer and the device shows "push broken, open the app".
- **TURN down:** LAN calls are unaffected; remote clients on restrictive networks fail media; the status endpoint and alert show it.
- **Derived-key mismatch after a key change:** the previous key is accepted during the overlap; clients refresh credentials on the next foreground and the auth-failure metric shows stragglers.
- **Credentials leaked:** a derived credential expires on its own (12 h web, 30 d mobile); revoking the device disables it at once in new registrations; the audit log shows the enrolment.
- **Video recording storage full or MinIO down:** new video recordings stop at the quota and a running one stops after 3 buffered fragments; audio recording is unaffected (S-42).
- **Video overload:** over `HELLO_VIDEO_MAX_SESSIONS` new video sections are refused (audio continues); inside a session, packets above the cap are dropped and counted.
- **App not updated:** the `v` field of the push and the REGISTER user agent let the server flag or refuse old apps; an older client keeps working on audio.

## Threat model

- **Account takeover through enrolment:** pairing codes are single-use, short-lived, hashed, rate limited and shown only to the authenticated owner; device creation is capped and audited; revocation is immediate for new registrations.
- **Toll fraud with a stolen client credential:** a client extension is a normal extension; outbound routes, call limits and trunk permissions apply as for phones; derived credentials expire; the CDR shows the device and its candidate type.
- **Cross-site WebSocket hijacking:** origin allow-list (S-8); credentials are never in the WebSocket URL.
- **TURN as a pivot into the LAN:** `denied-peer-ip` with a narrow `allowed-peer-ip` (S-22); quotas; credentials expire in an hour; the secret is sops-managed.
- **Signalling downgrade / media interception:** WSS only; DTLS fingerprints travel in signalling over TLS; the Kamailio to Hello hop is TLS (S-12) or the cleartext flag is explicit.
- **Push content exposure:** no secrets in payloads; caller info optional (S-34); tokens sealed at rest.
- **Replay of an old push:** `exp` and `ts` in the payload and `apns-expiration` shortly after sending.
- **Video recordings as sensitive data:** they show faces and rooms. Same access rules and audit as audio recordings, `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, object keys never exposed to the browser (the API streams them), a separate shorter retention (default 30 days), and the "including video" notice so both parties know.
- **Storage exhaustion by long video calls:** the per-call bitrate cap (`HELLO_VIDEO_MAX_KBPS`), the video quota and the 80 percent alert (S-42).
- **Denial of service by registration floods:** `pike`, per-IP and per-user limits, device caps, WebSocket frame and message size limits.

## RBAC and audit

- `user` role: manage own devices, push token, ring preferences, pairing codes, quality reports. `viewer` and `operator` see the Clients diagnostics; only `admin` changes `HELLO_*` policy through configuration. Admins and operators can revoke any device.
- Audit events (existing `audit_events`): `device.enrol`, `device.revoke`, `device.credentials` (count only), `push.bind`, `pairing.create`, `pairing.use`, `client.login`, `recording.video.download`, `recording.video.purge` (counts only), `recording.settings.update`.
